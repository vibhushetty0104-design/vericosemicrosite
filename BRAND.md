## Strategy

### Overview

Meridian Vascular is a Bengaluru-first guide to vein and leg-circulation health. It
explains symptoms, tests and treatment options in plain language and helps people
send an appointment request to vascular care, which a clinic must then confirm.

It began as an educational prototype that decoded venous Doppler reports and
explained the CEAP stages. Today it is a working demo, with fictional provider
profiles and a simulated appointment request, used to show the product to
potential partners. It is growing into a platform that will work with real,
verified vascular specialists and hospitals in Bengaluru.

What it really does is close the gap between noticing something in your legs and
walking into a consultation prepared. It turns an unfamiliar scan report, a
confusing set of treatment names and a worry about urgency into a short list of
facts, questions and next steps.

The structural problem: in India, vein care information comes mostly from people
selling a procedure (hospital landing pages, clinic ads) or from listing platforms
that rank doctors by ratings and fees. Patients rarely get neutral, readable
explanations before they are asked to decide.

- **Before:** "My legs feel heavy, the report says *GSV reflux 1.2 s at SFJ*, a
  clinic quoted a laser price, and I don't know if this is urgent."
- **After:** "I know what the report measures, what to ask, which signs mean go to
  emergency now, and I've sent a request to a specialist who will confirm a time."

Long-term ambition: to be the most trusted first stop for vascular care in
Bengaluru, and the partner reputable vascular teams want patients to arrive from.

### Audience

**Primary: adults in Bengaluru, roughly 30 to 65, with leg symptoms or visible
veins.** Many work standing jobs (teaching, retail, hospitality, healthcare,
traffic police, factory floors) or sit for long hours in IT and BPO jobs. Most
read English comfortably and a phone is their main screen. They want to know
whether this is serious, what the scan or the doctor will say, and roughly what
care involves.

- They already believe varicose veins are mostly cosmetic or "part of ageing",
  that surgery means a hospital stay, and that online doctor listings are paid
  placements.
- They are sceptical of clinic marketing, price "offers", miracle creams, and
  anything that sounds like a sales funnel.

**Secondary: family members researching for a parent or spouse.** Often the adult
child books the appointment. They read more, compare more, and want something they
can forward.

**Secondary: people already holding a venous duplex (Doppler) report.** They arrive
with a specific term to decode and are close to a decision.

**Secondary now: prospective partner vascular specialists and hospitals,** who see
the demo. They need to see that patients would reach them well informed, that their
profiles would be shown accurately, and that the demo never passes fiction off as
fact.

**Not for:**
- Anyone with sudden swelling in one leg, chest pain or breathlessness. They need
  emergency services (112 / 108), not this site.
- People looking for an online diagnosis, a prescription, or a guaranteed result.
- Cosmetic-only "vein removal" shoppers comparing discounts.
- Clinicians seeking continuing medical education.

### Positioning

**Category:** Independent vascular-care guide and appointment-request service for
Bengaluru patients.

**What it is not:** Not a hospital. Not a clinic. Not a doctor-rating marketplace.
Not a diagnostic tool. Not an insurance broker. Not a telemedicine provider.

**Competitive landscape:**
1. *Hospital and clinic service pages.* Detailed but written to sell a procedure;
   urgency and cost framing favour the provider.
2. *Doctor listing and booking platforms.* Broad coverage; star ratings, fees and
   "next available" slots dominate, and condition explanations are thin.
3. *General health content sites.* Readable but generic, often not local, and rarely
   explain an Indian Doppler report or the referral path.

Meridian Vascular sits between education and access: deeper than a listing
platform, more neutral than a hospital page, more local and practical than a
general health site.

**Structural differentials:**
- Focused on one body system (veins and leg circulation), explained end to end.
- Explains the patient's own documents (the Doppler report) rather than only the
  disease.
- Keeps education separate from referral: no ranking, no paid placement.
- Appointments are requests that a clinic must confirm. We never imply a slot is
  booked. In the demo, requests are simulated and say so.
- Urgent warning signs come first on every relevant page, ahead of anything that
  could look like marketing.

**Territory:** *clarity before the consultation.*

### Personality

- **Archetype:** The Trusted Explainer, a calm clinician-librarian who has time
  for your question.
- **Attributes:** Clarity, Care, Rigour, Restraint, Warmth, Locality.
- **What it is:** Calm. Precise. Plain-spoken. Unhurried. Respectful. Grounded in
  Bengaluru.
- **What it is not:** Not alarmist. Not salesy. Not clinical-cold. Not cute. Not
  hype-driven. Not falsely reassuring.

### References & Anti-References

These are trait references from general knowledge of each brand. None was studied
visually in this session.

#### References

- **NHS website (nhs.uk) condition pages:** Borrow the plain-English "when to get
  help" structure, putting urgent signs before everything else and defining terms
  in one line. Do not borrow its utilitarian blue or its government-form visual
  austerity.
- **A well-made printed field guide or anatomy atlas:** Borrow labelled line
  diagrams, generous margins and the habit of explaining one structure at a time.
  Do not borrow archaic ornament, sepia nostalgia or tiny caption type.
- **Quality long-form editorial publishing:** Borrow a confident serif headline
  voice and trust in the reader's patience for one good paragraph. Do not borrow
  paywalls, dense multi-column pages or opinion-led tone.

#### Anti-References

- **Hospital-chain procedure ads:** Reject "best surgeon in Bengaluru", "painless
  laser", "100% success", price "offers", countdowns and before/after galleries
  used as sales devices.
- **Doctor marketplace listings:** Reject star ratings, fee badges, "verified"
  ticks we cannot verify, and urgency built from "only 2 slots left".
- **Generic AI-made medical template:** Reject blue-to-teal gradients,
  stethoscope stock photos, emoji icons, pulsing "live" dots, mascots that become
  the main event, and stacks of interchangeable feature cards.

### Promise

- We explain before we refer.
- We say what we know, what we don't, and who can confirm it.
- We put warning signs first.
- We never present a request as a booking, a demo profile as a real doctor, or a
  guess as a diagnosis.

**Base message:** Meridian Vascular helps people in Bengaluru understand their vein
symptoms and reports, so they reach the right specialist prepared and on time.

**Synthesizing phrase:** Better clarity. Better-informed care.

### Guardrails

- **Tone summary:** Calm, precise, warm, and honest about uncertainty.
- **What the brand cannot be:** a clinic in disguise; a diagnosis engine; a
  ranking or review site; an insurance adviser; a source of fear; a mascot-led
  wellness app; anything that presents invented doctors, credentials,
  affiliations, ratings, prices or availability as real.
- **Litmus test:** *If a worried patient's family member could screenshot it and
  feel sold to, misled or frightened, it's wrong.*

## Voice

### Identity

We are Meridian Vascular. We explain what is happening in your veins and legs, in
the language you would use with a friend who happens to be a careful doctor. We
start with what you can feel and see, then walk through what a scan measures and
what treatment can involve.

We are not your doctor. Our job is to help you arrive at one prepared: knowing
which signs need emergency care today, which questions to ask, and what your
report is describing. When we are not sure, we say so. When something needs a
specialist to confirm, we say that too.

We are building this in Bengaluru, with the city's specialists and hospitals in
mind. While we demonstrate the service, the doctors you see are fictional and
labelled that way. When we launch with partners, we will only show real, verified
care teams, and when you ask for an appointment we will tell you exactly what
happens next.

**Essence:** We make vein care understandable before it becomes a decision.

### Tonal Rules

1. Lead with the answer, then the explanation. One idea per paragraph.
2. Write at a plain-English level on public pages. Define any clinical term in the
   same sentence or the next.
3. Use "may", "often", "can" for clinical statements; reserve "will" and "always"
   for process facts we control (for example, "a clinic will confirm your request").
4. Put urgent warning signs before any marketing, tool or call to action on a page.
5. Every clinical statistic, threshold or outcome needs a citation, or it does not
   ship.
6. Say who decides: "a vascular specialist can confirm", not "you have".
7. Never use fear to drive action. Describe risk factually, then give the next step.
8. Never describe an appointment as booked, scheduled or confirmed unless a clinic
   has confirmed it.
9. Name no real hospital, doctor, insurer, college, laboratory, accreditation body or
   society unless a verified relationship or a public, citable fact supports it.
   Demo records follow the same rule: fictional people get fictional or generic
   institutions.
10. Label AI-generated text as AI and remind readers it can be wrong.
11. Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Buttons say what happens next.
12. Address the reader as "you". Speak as "we".

**Identity boundaries:**
- We are not doctors talking down; we are explainers sitting beside you.
- We are not a clinic's marketing department.
- We are not a ratings site.
- We are not here to make you worry, or to make you feel falsely safe.

|---|---|

## Visual

### Logo & Marks

- **Wordmark:** "Meridian Vascular", set in the brand sans. In prose and alt text
  it is always two plain words, never "MERIDIAN—VASCULAR".
- **Symbol (provisional):** a single curved vein line with a node at each end,
  forest line on ivory or ivory line on forest, with terracotta nodes. Treat it as
  provisional until a designer finalises the mark and source files are versioned.
- **Clear space:** at least the height of the wordmark's capital M on every side.
- **Prohibitions:** never stretch, outline, add shadows or glows, place on a busy
  photograph, animate the symbol in a loop, or recolour it outside the core colours.
- **Source files:** none versioned yet (open item).

### Core Colors

- **Forest** `#0B4036` (mandatory). The brand's steadiness: the voice of care
  that has done this before. Carries authority without coldness.
- **Terracotta** `#CE5B40` (mandatory). The human warmth in the identity, and the
  single point of attention. Rare by design. It never signals danger.
- **Ivory** `#F7F5EF` (mandatory). The paper. Calm, warm, never hospital white.
- **Sage** `#8DAA9D` (optional). The quiet companion to Forest: secondary marks,
  rules and diagrams.
- **Pale sage** `#E4ECE6` (optional). A soft ground that groups related ideas
  without shouting.

Avoid: saturated hospital blues and teals; neon or emerald "live" greens; purple;
any red family used decoratively (red belongs to genuine urgency only, and is a
design-system decision, not an identity colour).

### Typefaces

Approved by the owner on 2026-10-21: Source Serif 4 for display, and a readable
sans (Plus Jakarta Sans) for body copy and controls.

- **Source Serif 4**, the editorial voice: display and headings, pull quotes.
  Fallback: `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif`. Licensing: believed SIL Open

- **Plus Jakarta Sans**, the reading and interface voice: body text, labels,
  buttons, forms. Fallback: `-apple-system, 'Segoe UI', Roboto, sans-serif`.
  Licensing: believed SIL OFL 1.1 (Google Fonts). **Not verified in this session.**
- **IBM Plex Mono**, measured clinical values only (for example "> 500 ms",
  "4.2 mm"). Fallback: `ui-monospace, Menlo, Consolas, monospace`. Licensing:
  believed SIL OFL 1.1. **Not verified in this session.**

Open item: Kannada and Hindi support. When local-language content is added, a
companion family with matching tone must be approved here first.

## Governance

### Naming

- The brand is "Meridian Vascular". Legacy names ("ClearPix", "Meridian Vascular
  Center") must not appear in any public surface.
- Features use plain descriptive names: "Symptom check", "Doppler report guide",
  "Treatment comparison", "Visit prep", "Educational guide".
- Do not name features after clinical acts we don't perform ("diagnosis",
  "triage", "consultation").
- New public names, partner names and any use of a hospital's or doctor's name
  require owner approval before shipping.

- Cure, guarantee, "permanent", "painless", "100%", "best", "top", "leading".
- Naming insurers, TPAs, hospitals, doctors or professional societies as
  endorsing, covering or partnering, without a verified relationship.
- Showing invented doctors, credentials, affiliations, ratings, fees, addresses,
  phone numbers or availability as if they were real. (Labelled demo content is
  permitted under Demo Content below.)
- Attaching real colleges, hospitals, laboratories, insurers, regulators (for
  example "NMC") or accreditation bodies to fictional people or places.
- "Medically reviewed" until a named, qualified reviewer and review date exist.
- Any claim of medical, legal or regulatory compliance (for example under India's
  DPDP Act, HIPAA, NABH or NMC rules).

### Demo Content

The product is shown to prospective partners as a demo. Fictional provider
profiles, sample fees, sample times and a simulated request flow are intended and
stay until verified partner records replace them. To keep that honest:

- Every fictional profile carries the label **"Demo profile · fictional"** next to
  the name, readable at phone size, never only in a footnote or tooltip.
- Every value on a demo profile says "sample" (sample fee, sample time, sample
  rating if ratings are ever shown).
- A simulated request ends with **"Demo request: not sent to any clinic"**, never a
  confirmation code or booking language.
- Demo records use clearly fictional institutions only (see Claims). The pattern
  is a generic type, a letter, a city and "(fictional)", for example "Demo Medical
  College A, Bengaluru (fictional)", "Demo Vascular Imaging Lab (fictional)", "Sample
  insurer note (demo)". Lettered names are used instead of invented proper names so
  that a demo name can never collide with a real institution.
- Degree and qualification types (MBBS, MS, MD, MCh, DNB) may appear on demo
  profiles under "Sample credentials". Named certifications, memberships,
  empanelments and registrations issued by real bodies may not.
- Demo records never appear in structured data, sitemaps or search previews as
  real providers.
- Emergency guidance, privacy notices and clinical evidence rules apply to demo
  pages exactly as they do to real ones.

### Accessibility Commitments

- **Target:** WCAG 2.2 Level AA on all public surfaces. This is a commitment, not a

- **Beyond the target:**
  - Urgent safety information is never hidden behind a tab, accordion, animation

  - Reduced-motion preferences are always honoured.
  - Primary pages are written in plain English, with terms defined in context.
- **Exceptions:** none tracked yet; exceptions will be logged alongside the audit
